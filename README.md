<h2 style = "text-align:center">A Machine Learning Model for Predicting Breast Cancer Recurrence using Clinical and Biomarker Data</h2>
Breast cancer recurrence remains a major clinical challenge, as it significantly impacts patient survival and quality of life. In this work, we propose an end-to-end machine learning (ML) framework for predicting breast cancer recurrence using a comprehensive, multi-modal dataset that integrates clinical, pathological, molecular, treatment, and longitudinal laboratory data. The dataset includes demographic features (age, sex), tumor characteristics (size, grade, stage, histology), biomarker and molecular profiles (ER, PR, HER2, BRCA status, Ki-67), treatment history (surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, hormone therapy), and tumor marker trends (CA 15-3 and CA 27-29 levels and slopes). After preprocessing, feature engineering, and encoding, we train and evaluate multiple ML models—including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and survival analysis approaches—using cross-validation and clinically relevant performance metrics such as AUC-ROC, F1-score, and concordance index. Our approach aims to assist oncologists by providing a risk score and interpretable insights (via SHAP feature importance) to guide personalized follow-up schedules and treatment planning. This work demonstrates the potential of combining bioinformatics and data science to deliver clinically meaningful, explainable, and data-driven decision support in precision oncology.
